## ¶1 — The hook

**Function:** Open with ChatGPT / generative AI diffusion. Raise the economic question.

**Drop-in text (adapted from your current ¶1–¶2 in Background):**

> The public release of ChatGPT in November 2022 marked a turning point in the diffusion of generative AI.  Within months, firms and workers began adopting generative AI tools to perform a growing range of tasks: drafting emails, writing code, summarizing documents, analyzing data.  When a new technology performs tasks that workers are paid to do, the net effect on their wages and employment is not obvious a priori: it depends on whether the productivity gains that accrue to them, through expanded output and rising demand for tasks complementary to generative AI, are large enough to offset the substitution pressure they bear directly.

## ¶3 — This paper

**Function:** State what you do, what you find. One paragraph.

**Draft (simple summary based on what's already in §3–§5):**

> This paper uses the Current Population Survey Annual Social and Economic Supplement (CPS-ASEC) for 2018--2025, linked to the occupation-level AI exposure scores of \textcite{eloundou2024gpts}, to estimate how generative AI has affected U.S.\ labor-market outcomes in the three years since ChatGPT's release.  An event-study difference-in-differences design exploiting cross-occupation variation in exposure delivers two main findings.  First, workers in high-exposure occupations experienced an increase in unemployment risk beginning in 2024, broadly shared across education groups.  Second, wage compression in high-exposure occupations is sharply asymmetric: it concentrates on non-college workers, while wages of college-educated workers in exposed occupations are statistically indistinguishable from those in less-exposed occupations.  A within-occupation specification further suggests that the education gap in the wage response reflects sorting across occupations on dimensions correlated with college share but not captured by the exposure score --- not an individual-level productivity gap within occupations.
